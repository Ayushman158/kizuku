Kizuku's calm is mostly space, so the space has to be deliberate. Everything below is a multiple of four, with one screen gutter that never varies.

The build currently gutters at 20, 22, 23 and 24 depending on the screen, which reads as drift rather than rhythm. One gutter of 20 on every screen; card padding 24; the only value that ever breaks the scale is the 42px control, which becomes 44.

The build uses 16, 18, 20, 21, 22 and 26 — six radii doing four jobs. Cards land on 20, groups on 16, and anything pill-shaped goes fully round rather than 26.

Shadows are tinted with the forest, never neutral black — on parchment a grey shadow reads as dirt. Flat for list rows, raised for input and home cards, lifted only for the action card, which is the one thing that should feel handed to you.

Three controls in the build sit under the 44pt floor: the mic at 36, the header icon buttons at 42, and the nav labels at 10px. None of them are hard to hit on a bench — all three are hard to hit one-handed on a train, which is where this app gets used.

Motion

ambient/drift

The landscape breathing sideways ±5px. Slow enough that you never catch it moving.

ambient/breath
3200ms · sine in-out · loop

The tree at 1 → 1.018. The plant is alive while you decide.

enter/soft
650ms · cubic out · 24px rise

How the prompt arrives. The only entrance in the product.

react/tap
260ms out, spring 9 / 130

The tree answering the watering can. Overshoots once, settles.

return/home
spring 12 / 120

Anything dragged and released. Never a linear snap back.

Every loop stops under prefers-reduced-motion and entrances resolve to their end state — the build already does this and it stays a rule, not a nicety. Nothing in Kizuku bounces, flashes or celebrates: the loudest moment in the product is a spring with a damping of 9.
